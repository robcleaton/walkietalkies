Woolwich

The Royal Arsenal turned riverside flats, with a free ferry that’s been crossing the Thames since 1889.

Woolwich grew up around one of Henry VIII's royal dockyards and went on to become one of Britain's great centres of military manufacturing at the Royal Arsenal — a history now being reworked into riverside flats, cultural venues and a free ferry that's been running since 1889.

History

People have lived on this stretch of the Thames since at least the Iron Age, but Woolwich's modern story begins in 1512, when Henry VIII founded a royal dockyard here to build his flagship. The Board of Ordnance began testing guns at the same site — known as the Warren — from the 1650s, and it grew into the Royal Arsenal, formally named in 1805, alongside the Royal Regiment of Artillery (formed 1716), the Royal Military Academy (1741) and vast barracks. At its Napoleonic-era peak the Arsenal, dockyard and garrison together employed thousands, and the population climbed from a few hundred around 1500 to over 40,000 by 1861. The dockyard closed in 1869, and the Arsenal itself finally shut in 1994, part of a long post-war industrial decline that also saw the closure of the Siemens cable works. Since then the old Arsenal site has been redeveloped as housing and a cultural quarter, alongside newer, harder history — the Thames Barrier, completed just downstream in 1984, and the murder of Lee Rigby near the barracks in 2013. Neighbouring Shooter's Hill, part of the same postcode, adds its own oddities: a Bronze Age burial mound, a Georgian folly built to commemorate a naval victory in India, and a road once so plagued by highwaymen it gave the hill its name.
